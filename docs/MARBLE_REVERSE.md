# How Marble (World Labs) makes a 3D world, and how we copy it on free GPUs (research, 28 Oct 2026)

Owner's request: "reverse engineer Marble ... forums, papers, open-source projects and models, so we don't think from scratch".

## 1. What is known about Marble (facts, with sources)
- **Output**: a 3D Gaussian splat world, the format it treats as highest quality. Full worlds are about 2 M splats and light ones about 500k. It also exports a collider mesh (coarse, for physics), a visual mesh, and videos along a camera path. Splats are rendered in the browser with **Spark**, World Labs' open THREE.js renderer. Sources: World Labs blog "Marble: A Multimodal World Model" (12 Nov 2025); [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/world-labs-formally-launches-marble-a-generative-world-model); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/12/fei-fei-lis-world-labs-speeds-up-the-world-model-race-with-marble-its-first-commercial-product/).
- **Inputs**: text, one image, several images (each assigned to a part of the scene), a short video, a **360° panorama**, or a coarse 3D layout drawn in the "Chisel" editor (walls and planes plus a style prompt).
- **Panorama**: a world can be started from an equirectangular panorama, and exports include a 2560×1280 pano ([invideo explainer](https://invideo.io/blog/world-labs-marble-3d-worlds/), [Marble how-to guide](https://www.brainandbot.ai/assets/download/handbooks/4-Marble-AI-HowTo-Guide-Feb2026.pdf)).
- **Speed**: a navigable preview takes about 30 s and a full world about 5 min.
- **Limits** (users): quality is best inside the area the input shows and falls off "more than a couple meters behind the camera". Adjacent rooms stay blank. "World Expansion" adds detail at the edges but is unreliable ([HN thread 1](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45902732), [HN thread 2](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45907541), [design4real test](https://design4real.de/en/world-labs-marble-practical-test/)).
- **Marble 1.1 / 1.1 Plus (Apr 2026)**: 1.1 Plus grows a world by "dynamic cubes" (up to 5 extra spatial units per generation). Exports are now roughly metric (real-world units) and offer OpenCV/OpenGL axes ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/world-labs-releases-marble-1.1-and-marble-1.1-plus)).
- **No paper**: World Labs has published no paper on Marble. RTFM (Oct 2025) is a different product: a real-time frame model (autoregressive video diffusion with a frame memory, no explicit 3D).

## 2. How Marble most likely works (our reading of the evidence)
These points are inferred, not published. Each is backed by the clues above and by open systems that match Marble's behaviour.
1. **Panorama first.** The input becomes a 360° panorama: pano input is accepted, a pano is exported, and the ~30 s preview fits a pano plus depth. This explains why the world is coherent in all directions but only good near the start point.
2. **Panorama → 3D.** Metric depth of the panorama gives a point cloud, which serves as the geometric "memory" of the scene.
3. **Expansion inside a bounded unit (a "cube").** New views along camera paths inside that unit are generated by a camera-controlled image/video model conditioned on renders of the point cloud. They fill what the pano could not see: behind objects and a step sideways. This is why quality drops a couple of metres out, and why "cubes" are the unit of growth.
4. **Reconstruction.** Splats are trained (about 2M) on the pano views plus the generated views, and a mesh is made from the splats' depth (TSDF), giving the collider and visual mesh.
5. **Expansion / 1.1 Plus**: the same loop is repeated at new centres ("cubes").

## 4. What our v1 run taught us, and the papers agree
- **v1** (drones painting one view at a time, world/20261027-1807) is the old "Text2Room / WonderWorld" style. Both papers above say it fails at full 360°. Ours: the first views were photoreal, but SDXL painted a new room into big holes (extra beds), and depth scales per view ran from 0.5 to 7.8.
- **Fixes the papers use**: (1) one coherent 360° panorama first; (2) prompts *without objects* plus separate ceiling and floor prompts; (3) one global point cloud as the geometry memory for every later view; (4) inpaint only what a sideways step uncovers (small gaps), not whole new walls; (5) 3DGS as the final model, trained on all views. That matches our run-4 splat quality on the views it has.

## 5. NAMMA World v2: the Recipe, on a Kaggle T4 (plan)
1. **Panorama** (≈2–3 min): photo → equirectangular canvas (FOV from K or MoGe). Anchored outpainting with RealVisXL inpainting + LCM. Ceiling and floor go first (120° views) with the photo copy on the back, then 8 × 85° views around. Prompts: room mood without objects, plus ceiling and floor prompts (a small VLM later; config text first). Then a refine pass. Upgrade to try later: HY-Pano-2-Qwen.
2. **Depth** (≈0.5 min): MoGe-2 on overlapping views of the pano (the `infer_panorama` method), scaled to the photo's own MoGe depth; optionally snapped to our room box (straight walls).
3. **Steps sideways** (≈4 min): cameras at the faces/corners of a ~1.5 m cube inside the room box. Render the pano mesh, inpaint only the uncovered gaps (dilated/closed masks, ControlNet depth, colour match), and use only those gap pixels.
4. **Splats** (≈4 min): gsplat (our run-4 trainer) on the pano views + gap views. Init from the point cloud, SH 0 (RGB), 5k steps, no opacity reset, depth + normal loss. Then a TSDF mesh for walking collisions, and the existing tour viewer.
5. **Check** as before: the input view equals the photo, real neighbouring frames give PSNR/SSIM, plus a 360° sheet. Synthetic room test first on the CPU.
Side tests (no Kaggle hours): FlashWorld via its ZeroGPU Space on the same two photos, as a quality yardstick. WorldMirror 2.0 / DA3 as fast camera solvers for the owner's home video (Plan A / Round 1).
